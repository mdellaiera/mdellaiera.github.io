About Me

I am a Research Fellow (postdoc) at the National University of Singapore (NUS), where I work on deep learning and image processing for remote sensing. My research focuses on developing novel and robust methods for understanding and combining information from heterogeneous imaging modalities, with a particular interest in Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) and optical imagery. I am particularly interested in SAR–optical image registration and fusion, multimodal representation learning, self-supervised learning, despeckling, and the development of physically informed machine-learning methods for remote sensing. I am also interested in the use of remote sensing and geophysical observations to study ionospheric dynamics, particularly Equatorial Plasma Bubbles (EPBs). This work combines SAR observations with GNSS measurements to investigate the propagation and evolution of ionospheric irregularities.

I received my PhD in Information and Communication Sciences and Technologies from Université Savoie Mont Blanc in 2024, where I worked at the Laboratoire d’Annecy de Physique des Particules (LAPP/CNRS) on deep learning methods for gamma-ray astronomy with the Cherenkov Telescope Array Observatory (CTAO). My doctoral research focused on deep learning for the reconstruction of very-high-energy gamma-ray events, with particular emphasis on domain adaptation and the transfer of models from simulations to real telescope observations.
